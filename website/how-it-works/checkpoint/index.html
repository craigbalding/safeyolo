One process, operator-owned

Each SafeYOLO instance runs one checkpoint process under your own user account, not root. Every agent connects through its own socket, and the checkpoint fixes the agent's identity when the connection is accepted.

What it checks

The checkpoint terminates TLS with the instance's own certificate authority and understands HTTP/1.1, HTTP/2, CONNECT tunnels and WebSockets. Depending on the request, it applies:

  • Network policy: allowed destinations, lists and lookalike-domain checks
  • Rate budgets and circuit breakers: limits on runaway loops and failing upstreams
  • Credential guard: detects secrets and checks where they are going
  • Service gateway: swaps the agent's scoped token for your real credential
  • Content inspection: pattern scanning, including prompt-injection signals on model traffic
  • Specialised paths: Docker API mediation, coding-client routing and SSH
  • Your own named checks: rules you write over the request's facts

Four answers

ResponseMeaning
AllowedThe request reaches its destination, and the decision is recorded
428Approval required: the request is outside policy, and you can approve it
403Denied by policy
429 or 503A rate budget or circuit breaker stopped it

What this gives you

  • A single place where every outbound request is decided
  • Policy validated before any agent can connect
  • No silent passthrough after a timeout or parse failure

What it does not do

  • Inspect streamed bodies or TLS passthrough as deeply as ordinary requests
  • Replace a destination's own authentication
