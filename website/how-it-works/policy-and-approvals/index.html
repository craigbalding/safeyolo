Policy is a file you control

policy.toml holds which hosts each agent may reach, its budgets, lists, grants and controls. safeyolo policy check validates a candidate without activating it, and safeyolo policy apply activates it and confirms the result. The running checkpoint reloads policy when the file changes.

Approvals arrive specific

A request outside policy returns 428 and records a pending approval with its exact scope: the agent, the destination and, for a service, the method and path. You decide in the CLI, in Command Centre on macOS, or through a delegated reviewer: a named agent that decides selected request types for named workers without gaining policy administration.

Answers that last as long as you choose

Service risk approvals can apply once, for the session, or be remembered. A grant never widens what a capability allows; it only approves risk inside what you already authorised.

Separate questions, by design

Network permission, credential permission, service authorisation, contract binding and risky-route approval are decided independently. Saying yes to one never quietly says yes to another.

Agents can propose, you adopt

An agent can submit a full proposed policy snapshot for your approval, and you review and adopt the exact change. Maintained host lists and signal feeds can update policy inputs, but receiving a signal never grants authority by itself.

What this gives you

  • Agents that work unattended inside the boundary you set
  • Questions only when something is genuinely outside it
  • Approvals you can delegate without handing over policy

What it does not do

  • Decide for you: an agent's request is a request until someone approves it
  • Widen a capability through a grant
